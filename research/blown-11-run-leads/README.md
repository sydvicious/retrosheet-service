# Blown leads

**Question:** which games did somebody blow an 11-run lead — and how many of them
was I at?

## Definition

A team **blew an 11-run lead** if it led by 11 or more runs at some point and
then gave *every run of it back* — the trailing team drew level or went ahead.
Whether the leader then recovered to win is reported, not filtered on: two of the
ten did. Requiring the leader to also lose is the stricter reading and gives 8
games; both counts are in `01-blown-leads.md`.

Ten games qualify, 1900–2025. The largest lead ever fully surrendered is 12 runs,
four times.

## Method

Two steps, Syd's:

1. **Prefilter on the game logs** for games where *both* teams finished with 11+
   runs. This is **lossless** for the question: if a team led by 11+ and the
   trailing team drew level, the trailing team finished with at least the
   leader's peak total (≥ 11), and the leader finished with at least its own peak
   too — so both finals must be ≥ 11. It cuts 201,874 games to 658 with
   play-by-play.
2. **Replay those games** from the `play` table, carrying the score after every
   play, and keep the ones where an 11+ run lead reached zero.

Candidates come from `game_log`, whose final scores are Retrosheet's own and are
treated as authoritative; only the in-game trajectory is reconstructed. Every
game in the results table has its reconstructed final checked back against the
game log.

The one query that can't use the prefilter is the denominator — how often an
11-run lead is held at all — because a lead that is never surrendered leaves the
other team well under 11. That one replays all 16M plays and takes ~40s, which is
essentially the whole runtime of the study.

## Generated vs hand-written

Generated by `run.ts` (don't hand-edit; edit `queries.ts` instead):

- `01-blown-leads.md` — the ten games, with where the lead peaked, where it
  vanished, and how each ended. Games Syd attended are starred.
- `02-near-misses.md` — 10-run leads given up entirely. Also the safety net: a
  game whose reconstruction came up a run short lands here rather than in the
  main table, so this list is eyeballed every run.
- `03-data-quality.md` — the run-derivation evidence, the two parser bugs, and
  the play-by-play coverage gap.

`FINDINGS.md` is interpretation and is never regenerated.

## Scope

- **All game types.** Unlike the other studies this does *not* filter to the
  regular season — the question is about any baseball game. No postseason or
  All-Star game turns out to qualify, so the choice doesn't move the answer.
- **1900–2025**, the extent of Retrosheet's play-by-play.

## What this study cannot see

462 of the 1,120 candidate games have a game log but no play-by-play, so their
trajectory is unknowable here: 404 of them are pre-1900 (there is no event data
at all before 1900), and the rest trail off through 1955. Anything on that list
would have to be checked by hand from another source.

## Don't trust `play.runs_on_play`

The mart's stored run column disagrees with the official final score in **13% of
games**, in both directions, from two parser bugs (see `03-data-quality.md`).
This study re-derives runs from the raw event string — `RUNS` in `queries.ts` —
which lifts final-score agreement from 86.7% to 98.8%.

That correction is not cosmetic: it removes two false positives (Jun 8, 1989 and
Aug 21, 1990, both really 10-run leads inflated by a phantom run) and recovers
one false negative (Sep 4, 2002, where a dropped run hid the ninth-inning tie).
Any future study that turns on the score *at a moment* rather than at the end
should do the same until the parser is fixed — see the verification bullet under
"Future — full game-state analysis" in the project plan.
